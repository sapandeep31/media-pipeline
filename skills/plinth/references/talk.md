# Talk mode

For keynotes, internal updates, classes and board meetings.

## Pick the shape first
- **Update or board meeting:** answer first. Slide 2 states the conclusion and the decision you need; the rest is evidence. This is Barbara Minto's pyramid: conclusion, then grouped reasons, then data.
- **Persuasion or keynote:** contrast. Alternate "what is" with "what could be" and end on the new state. Nancy Duarte mapped this shape across Jobs' 2007 iPhone launch and King's "I Have a Dream".
- **Teaching:** one concept per slide, then an example slide, then a check question.

## Patterns worth stealing
- **Lessig / Takahashi method:** one large word or number per slide at a fast cadence. Good for openings.
- **Jobs keynote:** a dark slide with one number or one product image while the speaker talks. The slide never repeats what is said.
- **Slidedoc:** when the deck will be read without a presenter, write fuller sentences, but still one claim per page.
- **Assertion-evidence (Michael Alley, Penn State):** a sentence headline plus visual evidence. In his engineering-class studies students recalled more than with topic-plus-bullets slides.

## Length
About 1 slide per minute for dense material, up to 2 per minute for image-led talks. The appendix goes after the closing slide, not in the flow.

## Closing slide
Not "Thank you" and not "Questions?". End on the claim you want remembered, plus a contact line.
