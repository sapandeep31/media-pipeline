# Scenarios: spines by occasion

Pick the row that matches, copy its spine, then replace each "must claim" with the user's actual claim. Timings are speaking time; the total includes a small buffer. A spine is a starting shape, not a form to fill: merge or drop slides when a claim is not true yet, never pad one with filler.

Rule of thumb for pace: 20 to 30 seconds per content slide, 5 to 10 for a title or section break. Over that, the slide has two jobs.

---

## 1. Hackathon video, 3 minutes

Judges watch hundreds. They decide in the first 20 seconds. Detailed guidance in `pitch.md`.

| # | Eyebrow | The headline must claim | s |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | none | Product name + who it is for + what changes for them | 10 |
| 2 | Problem | A named user loses a named amount (time, money, risk) | 20 |
| 3 | Why now | The change that makes this buildable this year | 15 |
| 4 | Product | What the user sees at the aha moment | 15 |
| 5 | Demo | The flow works end to end (3 steps, real tx if on-chain) | 45 |
| 6 | How it works | What runs where, and why that choice | 15 |
| 7 | Traction | The most real signal you have, even if small | 15 |
| 8 | Go-to-market | Where the first 100 users come from | 15 |
| 9 | Team | Why these people can finish it | 15 |
| 10 | Ask | What you want next and what it unlocks for users | 10 |

Total 175 s. Never drop Demo or Team.

## 2. Live demo day, 5 minutes

A room, a clock, a projector that may fail. Record a backup video of the demo.

| # | Eyebrow | Must claim | s |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | none | One-liner | 15 |
| 2 | Problem | The pain, told through one real person | 30 |
| 3 | Product | What it does in one sentence | 15 |
| 4 | Demo | Live; narrate the outcome, not the clicks | 90 |
| 5 | Traction | Growth as a line, with dates | 30 |
| 6 | Market | Bottom-up: buyers x price | 30 |
| 7 | Business model | Who pays, how much, how often | 20 |
| 8 | Team | Relevant proof per founder | 20 |
| 9 | Ask | Amount, use, milestone it reaches | 20 |

Total 270 s + 30 s buffer.

## 3. Seed investor meeting, 10 minutes of deck

Expect interruptions; the deck is a script for a conversation. Send a leave-behind version with more text.

| # | Eyebrow | Must claim | min |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | none | Company + one-liner | 0.3 |
| 2 | Problem | Size and frequency of the pain, sourced | 1 |
| 3 | Solution | How the product removes it | 1 |
| 4 | Why now | The shift that opens the window | 0.7 |
| 5 | Product | Screenshot of the core loop | 1 |
| 6 | Traction | The strongest metric, with its trend | 1 |
| 7 | Market | Bottom-up SAM, then the path to larger | 1 |
| 8 | Business model | Unit economics or the pricing hypothesis | 0.8 |
| 9 | Competition | What you do that others structurally cannot | 0.8 |
| 10 | Go-to-market | The repeatable channel, with a number | 0.8 |
| 11 | Team | Why this team wins this market | 0.8 |
| 12 | Ask | Round size, runway, the milestones it buys | 0.8 |

Appendix slides (financials, cohort data, roadmap) after the ask, not in the flow.

## 4. Accelerator application (YC-style, written + 1-minute video)

Usually no slides at all, or a deck the partner skims in 60 seconds. Every slide must survive being read without you.

| # | Eyebrow | Must claim |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | none | What you make, in words a stranger understands |
| 2 | Problem | Who has it and how you know (you talked to N of them) |
| 3 | Product | It exists: a screenshot or link |
| 4 | Progress | What happened since you started, with dates |
| 5 | Insight | What you know that others in the space do not |
| 6 | Team | How long you have known each other and what you built together |
| 7 | Ask | Why this program, now |

Video: founders on camera, 60 s, no music, no slides needed.

## 5. Grant proposal (foundation, ecosystem, research fund)

Grant reviewers score against published criteria. Mirror their rubric in your eyebrows.

| # | Eyebrow | Must claim | min |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | none | Project + the public good it produces | 0.5 |
| 2 | Need | Who lacks this today, with evidence | 1 |
| 3 | Fit | Which of the funder's stated priorities this serves, by name | 0.7 |
| 4 | Approach | What you will build and why this way | 1 |
| 5 | Milestones | 3 to 5 deliverables, each dated and checkable | 1 |
| 6 | Budget | Amount per milestone; what each dollar pays for | 1 |
| 7 | Team | Past delivery on similar work, with links | 0.7 |
| 8 | Impact | How success is measured and reported | 0.7 |
| 9 | Sustainability | What happens after the grant runs out | 0.5 |

Deliverables must be verifiable ("open-source SDK on npm with 3 example apps"), never "raise awareness".

## 6. Sales / customer pitch, 15 to 20 minutes

About them, not you. The first slide that mentions your product is slide 3 at the earliest.

| # | Eyebrow | Must claim | min |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | none | Their outcome, in their words: "Cut [Client]'s payout wait from 2 days to 2 hours" | 0.5 |
| 2 | What we heard | Their situation as they described it in discovery | 2 |
| 3 | Cost of today | What the current way costs them per month | 2 |
| 4 | Approach | How the product changes that specific process | 2 |
| 5 | Demo | Their data or their workflow, not a generic one | 5 |
| 6 | Proof | A similar customer's before and after, named | 2 |
| 7 | Rollout | Weeks 1 to 4: who does what | 2 |
| 8 | Price | Price next to the cost from slide 3 | 1 |
| 9 | Next step | One concrete action with a date | 0.5 |

## 7. Internal quarterly update, 15 minutes

Lead with the verdict. Leadership wants to know if they need to act.

| # | Eyebrow | Must claim | min |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Q3 in one line | "We hit 2 of 3 goals; retention missed by 4 points" | 1 |
| 2 | Goal 1 | Result vs target, with the reason | 2 |
| 3 | Goal 2 | Same | 2 |
| 4 | Goal 3 | Same; misses get the most honest slide | 2 |
| 5 | What we learned | The one thing that changes how we work | 2 |
| 6 | Next quarter | 3 goals with numbers and owners | 3 |
| 7 | Decisions needed | What you need from this room, by when | 2 |

## 8. Board meeting, 30 to 60 minutes (deck pre-read, meeting discusses)

Send it 48 hours ahead. In the room, skip to decisions.

| # | Eyebrow | Must claim |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Summary | Three sentences: state of the business, biggest risk, the ask |
| 2 | KPIs | Table vs plan and vs last quarter; flag variances over 10% |
| 3 | Cash | Runway in months at current and planned burn |
| 4 | Wins | What went better than planned and why |
| 5 | Concerns | What went worse and what you are doing about it |
| 6 | Strategic topic | The one question you want the board's judgment on |
| 7 | Decisions | Resolutions for a vote, stated exactly |
| 8 | Appendix | Financials, hiring, pipeline detail |

## 9. Product launch keynote, 20 to 30 minutes

A story with a reveal. One new idea per section; the demo is the peak.

| # | Eyebrow | Must claim | min |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | none | The date and the one-line promise | 0.5 |
| 2 | Where we are | What users do today and what annoys them | 3 |
| 3 | The idea | The principle behind the new thing | 2 |
| 4 | Reveal | Name + hero image, nothing else on the slide | 1 |
| 5-7 | Feature 1-3 | Each: what you can now do, shown in a demo | 12 |
| 8 | Proof | A beta user's result, named | 2 |
| 9 | Availability | Price, date, where to get it | 1.5 |
| 10 | Close | The promise from slide 1, now true | 0.5 |

## 10. Conference talk, 25 to 40 minutes

The audience chose to be there to learn one thing. Say it at the start and the end. Detailed guidance in `talk.md`.

| # | Eyebrow | Must claim | min |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | none | Title as a claim + your name | 0.5 |
| 2 | The question | What the audience wondered before walking in | 2 |
| 3 | The answer | The finding, stated up front | 1 |
| 4-12 | Evidence | One slide per supporting point, each with its data or example | 18 |
| 13 | Caveats | Where this does not hold | 2 |
| 14 | Try this | What the audience does on Monday | 2 |
| 15 | Close | The answer again + where to find the slides | 0.5 |

Leave 5 to 10 minutes for questions if the slot allows.

## 11. Teaching / lecture, 45 to 90 minutes

Headlines are statements students can write down and revise from. Check understanding every 10 to 15 minutes.

| Block | Slides | Must claim |
|---|---|---|
| Open | 1-2 | What students will be able to do by the end (a verb: calculate, explain, build) |
| Hook | 1 | A question or case they cannot answer yet |
| Concept 1 | 3-5 | Definition, worked example, common mistake |
| Check | 1 | A question to answer in pairs, no answer on the slide |
| Concept 2, 3 | 3-5 each | Same pattern |
| Synthesis | 1-2 | How the concepts connect, as a diagram |
| Close | 1 | The hook question, now answerable + what to do before next class |

Denser slides are acceptable here because students read them later, but put the extra text in handout or notes, not the projected slide.

## 12. Thesis / skripsi defense, 15 to 20 minutes

The committee has read the document. They want to see that you understand your own work. Follow the campus format if one is given (see the `pedoman` / `atmajaya` skills for UAJM).

| # | Eyebrow | Must claim | min |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | none | Title, name, student ID, supervisors | 0.5 |
| 2 | Latar belakang / Background | The gap, with one sourced number | 2 |
| 3 | Rumusan masalah / Research question | The exact question(s), numbered as in the document | 1 |
| 4 | Tujuan / Objective | What the research set out to produce | 0.5 |
| 5 | Metode / Method | Design, data, tools, in one diagram | 3 |
| 6-8 | Hasil / Results | One result per slide, answering each question in order | 5 |
| 9 | Pembahasan / Discussion | What the results mean vs prior work | 2 |
| 10 | Kesimpulan / Conclusion | One sentence per research question | 1 |
| 11 | Saran / Limitations and future work | What you would do differently | 1 |

Put the numbers from your results chapter on the slides exactly as they appear in the document; examiners check. Prepare backup slides for the method details you expect to be questioned on.

## 13. Lightning talk / Pecha Kucha / Ignite, 5 minutes, 20 slides

Slides auto-advance every 15 seconds. One image or one phrase per slide; the story is in your voice.

| Slides | Must claim |
|---|---|
| 1 | Title as a claim |
| 2-4 | The situation everyone recognizes |
| 5-7 | The complication: what went wrong or what you noticed |
| 8-15 | The journey or evidence, one beat per slide |
| 16-18 | What you learned |
| 19 | What the audience should try |
| 20 | Closing line + handle |

Rehearse with the timer on; a 15-second slide holds about 35 spoken words. Headlines can drop to 3 to 6 words here.

## 14. All-hands, 30 to 60 minutes (several speakers)

Many presenters, one deck. Agree on the template and eyebrows before anyone builds slides.

| Block | Slides | Must claim |
|---|---|---|
| Open | 1 | The one thing everyone should remember from today |
| Company numbers | 2-3 | Where we stand vs the goal, one chart each |
| Team spotlights | 2 per team | What shipped and who shipped it, by name |
| Customer moment | 1 | A real customer's words or story |
| Big decision / change | 2-3 | What changes, why, and what it means for each person |
| Welcome | 1 | New people, with name and role |
| Q&A | 1 | Collected questions answered, including the hard ones |
| Close | 1 | The opening line again |

Keep each speaker to one eyebrow color or label so the audience knows whose section they are in.
